\# Insights \& Recommendations



\## Executive Summary

Olist is effective at acquiring new customers, but structurally weak at retaining

them — and the customers it is losing were, on average, just as valuable as its

top-tier VIP customers. Retention, not acquisition, is the single highest-leverage

opportunity for revenue growth.



\---



\## 1. Geography — Demand Is Concentrated, But So Is Delivery Cost



\*\*Finding:\*\* São Paulo alone accounts for \~15.4K of \~99K valid orders (\~15.6% of

total order volume), while 26.83% of cities that placed any order did so exactly

once.



\*\*Why it matters:\*\* This is a classic urban-concentration pattern in Brazilian

e-commerce — demand clusters around a handful of metro areas (São Paulo, Rio de

Janeiro, Belo Horizonte), with a long tail of low-frequency, geographically

scattered demand.



\*\*Business impact:\*\* These two groups have very different costs to serve. Dense

metro orders are cheaper and faster to fulfill; long-tail orders are more

expensive and, per the satisfaction analysis below, likely to arrive later —

directly hurting the experience of exactly the customers hardest to serve well.



\*\*Recommendation:\*\* Prioritize logistics investment (regional warehousing or

local carrier partnerships) in the São Paulo–Rio–Minas Gerais corridor, where

ROI is clearest. For single-order long-tail cities, evaluate whether premium

shipping is cost-justified before investing further. \*Success metric:\*

reduction in average delivery delay for the non-metro order segment.



\---



\## 2. Sales \& Product — Seller Concentration Is a Retention Risk \*and\* an Opportunity



\*\*Finding:\*\* The top 5 sellers alone generated \~1.05M BRL (\~7.7%) of total

revenue (13.59M BRL), out of thousands of active sellers.



\*\*Why it matters:\*\* A classic power-law seller distribution — expected in any

marketplace, but worth actively monitoring rather than assuming it's benign.



\*\*Business impact:\*\* If even one top seller leaves the platform, the revenue

impact is disproportionate. Conversely, understanding what makes these sellers

successful (category mix, fulfillment speed, pricing) is a replicable playbook

for mid-tier sellers.



\*\*Recommendation:\*\* Launch a Key Account Management program for the top 5-10

sellers to reduce churn risk, paired with a qualitative study of what

differentiates them. \*Success metric:\* top-5-seller revenue share trending

down over time (i.e., growth becoming more broadly distributed).



\---



\## 3. Customer Satisfaction — Cancellations Signal Early Churn Risk



\*\*Finding:\*\* Cancellation rate is highest among repeat customers —

At-Risk/Hibernating (0.18%) and VIP/Top (0.15%) — and lowest among

One-time/Churned customers (0.05%). Separately, delivery delay correlates

strongly and monotonically with review score: 21 days average delay for a

1-star review vs. 11 days for a 5-star review.



\*\*Why it matters:\*\* This is counter to the naive assumption that one-time

buyers cancel most. It suggests that when a repeat, high-value customer

experiences a cancellation, it may be a pivotal moment in the relationship —

not background noise. \*(Note: this is a correlational pattern observed in the

BI layer, not a proven causal claim.)\*



\*\*Business impact:\*\* A cancellation for a repeat customer carries a much

higher opportunity cost than for a one-time buyer, since it risks an entire

future revenue stream, not a single transaction.



\*\*Recommendation:\*\* Build an operational trigger: any time a VIP or At-Risk

customer's order is canceled, automatically flag it for proactive customer

support outreach before the relationship is lost. \*Success metric:\*

repeat-purchase rate within 90 days for customers who received this

intervention vs. those who didn't.



\---



\## 4. Customer Segmentation (RFM) — Retention, Not Value, Is the Real Gap



\*\*Finding:\*\* Only 3.06% of customers make repeat purchases. Average customer

value is nearly identical between VIP/Top (261.91 BRL) and At-Risk/Hibernating

(253.34 BRL) customers.



\*\*Why it matters:\*\* The customers slipping toward churn (At-Risk) are not

lower-value customers — they were, on average, worth almost exactly what

current VIPs are worth. The gap isn't a value problem; it's an

engagement/retention problem.



\*\*Business impact:\*\* At current segment sizes, \~1,325 At-Risk customers ×

\~253 BRL average value represents approximately 335K BRL of historical

customer value at risk of being fully lost — recoverable at zero acquisition

cost, since these customers are already acquired.



\*\*Recommendation:\*\* Launch a targeted win-back campaign specifically for the

At-Risk segment. More strategically: because the platform-wide repeat rate is

just 3.06%, retention is the single highest-leverage lever for long-term

revenue growth. \*Success metric:\* % of At-Risk customers who make at least

one more purchase within 90 days of the campaign.
